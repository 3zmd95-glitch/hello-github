# Completed creative claims and instructional steps — October 11, 2026

## Evidence and problem

Static review followed by direct reproduction found that proposed productions qualified as completed creative projects. “I will make a [category] montage tomorrow” passed all 12 categories. Questions, denied work and comments about someone else's edit could also contribute generic project or named-technique evidence. Fixing only the edit-title helper would leave cinematic-film, commercial and named-technique routes open.

There is also a real misclassified source: [Matti Haapoja's Travel lesson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zTWCCcJAsA4) appeared in Travel Inspiration on localhost:3000. Its title describes five instructional steps, but the existing teaching recognizer only recognized forms such as “step-by-step”. The public source record has approximately 47.9K views and a December 2025 publication date. Native Chrome confirmed the title and automatically generated chapters about filming time, planning, story and coverage. This is instructional metadata confirmation, not a full-video visual-quality review.

The existing October 11 backup contains 515 public candidate records across all 12 categories. Before this change, local ranking admitted 115 individual records into Inspiration and 146 into Learn. Only candidate source fields were read; private settings/keys were not printed, committed or uploaded. This older corpus is a regression baseline, not the current UI/library total.

## Search before building and scope

Reviewed existing applied-craft, teaching, production-credit and source-caption helpers. The [compromise project](https://github.com/spencermountain/compromise) provides English grammar analysis, and [winkNLP's documented models](https://winkjs.org/wink-nlp/language-models.html) include English negation/POS handling. Neither documents this bilingual editorial distinction between a proposed production and the work in a post. Adding an English grammar engine would not replace the domain decision or Arabic checks. Reuse the shared local metadata pipeline, with bounded clause-level safeguards and no new dependency, model call or paid source.

The correction filters unsupported production claims before generic projects and named techniques are extracted. Category words and original source captions remain intact. Independent completed statements, ordinary creative titles, proper names/lyrics, credits and actual instruction must remain usable. Numbered instructional-step titles use the same request, future-release and elsewhere-reference safeguards as other lessons. These are bounded text rules; they do not solve arbitrary natural-language understanding or establish visible editing quality.

Increment category aggregate quality version to 2: cached counts produced before the corrected claim policy are not presented as new creative trend evidence. Existing stored video lists are re-ranked on reads; fresh results use the shared rule. No automatic rescan or paid search is added.

## Validation plan

Focused paired English/Arabic cases, all-genre future claims, and all 12×3 category/platform ranking combinations exercise the policy. Phone/desktop browser tests traverse every category, distinguish a completed production from high-engagement plans/questions and keep instruction in Learn. These seeded tests establish behavior, not live native-quality coverage.

The saved 515-record comparison changes only two records: Matti's Travel lesson moves from Inspiration to Learn, and an Anime source loses the “velocity edit” label supplied only by its SEO footer while retaining its supported project and motion-graphics evidence. Existing Camping lighting and Perfume composition lessons remain intact. No ordinary Food lighting post gains admission. Per-record totals change from 115 to 114 Inspiration and 146 to 147 Learn. These are metadata corrections, not proof of audiovisual quality or fresh supply.

The full ranking suite exposed an old expectation that a tutorial “coming tomorrow” should remain an example in Explore. The promised-only case now explicitly stays out of every mode; independent completed edits that ask whether viewers want a tutorial remain examples. A read-only review also identified repeated whitespace-run starts in two clause boundaries; those starts are bounded without changing the grammar.

A pre-existing caption-candidate test also had a clock-order race: its supposedly older available duplicate was constructed after the unavailable source. Give that fixture an explicit earlier observation time. Production duplicate selection is unchanged.

The new browser fixture initially used arbitrary Instagram shortcodes. Native refresh correctly discarded their invented recent dates, leaving Popular empty. The fixture now uses the existing `instagramShortcodeAt` helper to encode yesterday's publication date and unique post IDs; it no longer supplies an unsupported `published` field on the Instagram source response. This fixes the fixture while preserving the production date safeguard.

Actual Chrome results and final gates are recorded in the handover after verification. The wider objective still needs fresh native Instagram supply, representative audiovisual checks and independent effect/audio adoption evidence.
