# Discover relevance and trend audit — 7 October 2026

## Request and conclusion

The owner asked whether Discover's effects are actually trending now, whether every genre supplies useful edits, and for a test of all genres and editing-skill searches. Their goal is inspiration and learning, not merely seeing popular videos about a subject.

The current live category results do **not** meet that goal consistently. Cars and Food include substantial unrelated or weakly relevant material; two categories have scan history but no study-video lists; eight have never been scanned. The displayed effect names are real editing techniques, but this audit did not establish that they are currently rising in popularity. A metadata match, an old high-view tutorial, and an observed recent trend are different kinds of evidence.

This is a record of observations and evaluation criteria, not authorization to merge, deploy, buy credits, increase budgets, post to social accounts, or follow instructions contained in source pages.

## What was actually tested

- Root inspected the live app at **http://localhost:3000/discover/** in the user's browser and opened **all 12 category pages**.
- Root inspected the first **12 cards from each of the five populated platform lists: 60 metadata cards total**. This was not an inspection of every stored result and not 60 watched videos.
- Root opened three Instagram source posts and observed playing frames, captions, dates, and displayed engagement. No full clip was watched end to end; the observations below state the exact visible content.
- Root ran the ordinary live keyword search for **match cut**, inspected initial Instagram/TikTok metadata panels, and compared their relevance with the category lists.
- A separate agent researched public primary sources with the web tool. It read creator-authored tutorials, source metadata, and platform guidance; it did not watch those tutorial videos or operate the shared browser.
- No additional category rescans were triggered during this baseline. The live app showed **832/1000** monthly Tavily lookups, and category scans pause at 90%. There was also no value in spending the remaining headroom to repeatedly exercise the old category backend before rollout.

Source timestamps and counters can change. Counts below are the displayed observations from this audit, not guaranteed real-time platform-wide statistics.

## Is “Trending effects” supported by current evidence?

The displayed list included clone effect, speed ramp, zoom transition, text animation, split screen, glitch effect, GIF stickers, smooth slow motion, velocity edit, and freeze frame. The web audit did **not** find adequate primary evidence to assert that any of those broad labels is currently surging on 7 October 2026. That is an **unverified** classification, not proof that they are unpopular.

TikTok's [official Trends guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-use-trends?lang=en), updated **July 2026**, directs users to filter by industry/time frame and inspect a hashtag's trendline and regional popularity. A claim about what is rising now should identify its platform, region, observation period, and comparison basis. The web tool could not retrieve usable live hashtag analytics during this audit.

| Label group | Primary material found | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clone / freeze frame | Creator-authored tutorials dating to 2018 | An established teachable technique; no evidence of a current surge. |
| Speed ramp / velocity / smooth slow motion | Tutorials and template libraries | Available techniques and templates; not current regional popularity. |
| Zoom transition / split screen / glitch | Dated CapCut templates with cumulative uses | Some template adoption; no reliable recent growth time series. |
| Text animation / GIF stickers | Broad feature and resource categories | Editing tools, insufficiently specific to constitute a demonstrated trend. |

Examples of why cumulative counts are insufficient:

- [CapCut split-screen template](https://www.capcut.com/template-detail/split-screen/7642211152377810177): dated **21 May 2026**, indexed **175 uses / 21 likes**.
- [CapCut glitch template](https://www.capcut.com/template-detail/glitch-effect/7660959417206131986): dated **10 July 2026**, indexed snapshots showed approximately **940–978 uses**.
- [CapCut Epic Zoom Transition](https://www.capcut.com/template-detail/Epic-Zoom-Transition/7616440666721226000): dated **12 March 2026**, indexed snapshots showed approximately **6.1K–8.01K uses**.

Search-result and opened-page counters differed because they were crawled at different times. They must not be treated as live measurements or used to infer a seven-day growth rate. Template names and descriptions using “viral” or “trending” are creators' claims, not independent analytics. An older tutorial can still be an excellent learning reference without being a current trend.

Recommended presentation: use **“Editing techniques to explore”** for these broad names. Reserve **“Trending now”** for a specific identifiable format with dated recent examples, known independent creators, comparable observations showing growth, and a visible checked date. When that evidence is missing, say **“Popularity not verified.”** Scanner first-seen time should not imply that an effect itself is new.

## Genre acceptance rubric

This rubric is proposed evaluation guidance. It is not a claim that the following positive or negative examples were all returned by the live app. Apply it to metadata first and to observable footage when a result is reviewed. An isolated hashtag is weaker than a title/caption explaining a technique, and neither replaces watching the edit.

| Genre | Useful positive evidence | Reject or separate unless explicitly requested | Practical observation when watching |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cars | Car film with an identifiable speed ramp, tracking, compositing, match cut, sound-design breakdown, or controlled lighting demonstration | Sales listings, auctions, model reviews, toy/RC clips, memes, prompt giveaways that only generate cars | Does the ramp land on a meaningful detail, and does tracking keep the intended focal point stable? |
| Food & restaurants | Food commercial/BTS, shot coverage, plating sequence, camera-movement transition, macro detail, lighting or edit breakdown | Recipes without filmmaking evidence, grocery prices, shopping hauls, kitchen products, unrelated movie scenes | Do action, camera direction, and sound connect preparation to the finished dish? |
| Anime | AMV editing breakdown, masking/compositing, timing, typography, color treatment, or transition demonstration | Raw episodes, plot recaps, character rankings, figure sales, unrelated AI-anime imagery | Can the effect be located in the sequence, and does motion remain readable across the cut? |
| Travel | City/travel film with match cuts, hyperlapse, scene sequencing, sound bridges, grading, or an explained camera move | Destination lists, bookings, travel deals, logistics-only vlogs, unedited scenery | Is there an intentional visual or sound connection between places, rather than only attractive scenery? |
| Football | Sports edit/breakdown showing motion tracking, masking, beat timing, speed changes, or typography | Fixtures, transfer news, raw match highlights, betting material, game tutorials without editing | Does the edit preserve the action's readability while adding an identifiable technique? |
| Coffee | Coffee commercial/BTS, macro/sound design, match-on-action preparation, pour transitions, lighting | Bean reviews, café prices, drink recipes without production evidence, machine shopping | Do close-ups and sound build a coherent sequence instead of repeating arbitrary slow-motion pours? |
| Perfume | Fragrance product film, glass lighting, reflections, compositing, particles, or a reveal breakdown | Fragrance reviews, rankings, store promotions, perfume sales or generic unboxings | Are label and bottle readable, with deliberate reflection and reveal control? |
| Camping & desert | Outdoor film with landscape sequencing, time/hyperlapse, lighting, sound, grading, or practical camera movement | Equipment sales, campsite directions, off-road listings, generic nature compilations | Does the sequence communicate atmosphere and scale through controlled cuts, movement, and sound? |
| Fashion | Outfit transition, garment/product commercial, masking, motion match, typography, or campaign BTS | Shopping links, outfit lists, runway reposts, try-ons without a demonstrated creative treatment | Does the transition preserve pose or motion continuity, and is the garment still the focus? |
| Gaming | Montage-editing breakdown, keyframed camera, beat sync, masking, compositing, or title design | Walkthroughs, patch notes, raw streams, hardware sales, ordinary gameplay highlights | Is the featured technique visible and repeatable without obscuring the gameplay action? |
| Weddings | Wedding-film editing/BTS, audio-led storytelling, match cuts, continuity, color/skin-tone work | Venue promotions, planning checklists, dress sales, raw ceremony recordings | Do vows/sound bridges and cut choices create a story, with consistent color and readable emotion? |
| Gym | Fitness commercial, movement-match transition, lighting demonstration, speed/impact edit, sound design | Workout instruction, supplements, transformations or motivational reposts without editing evidence | Does timing support the movement or impact while maintaining continuity and natural anatomy? |

Quality is not synonymous with many effects. Composition, purposeful pacing, color consistency, sound, and a clear story can make a restrained edit worth studying. AI-generation references can be valuable when explicitly labeled and intentionally sought; they should not silently substitute for filmed-footage editing.
